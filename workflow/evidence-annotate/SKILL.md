---
name: evidence-annotate
description: Burn touches, step captions, boxes, zoom and privacy masks into a screen recording or a still. Use to annotate a clip, make an annotated still, show what a test or bot did on screen, or mask private information (account names, numbers, e-mails) before a recording or screenshot is shared.
---

`scripts/annotate.py` re-encodes a recording with key and typing pills, click rings (a press and a drag's release), drag trails, a drawn pointer (a fingertip dot or a mouse arrow), numbered step captions, labelled boxes, an eased zoom into a region, and blacked-out private regions. The project supplies what is specific to it: the events, the steps, the masks. Needs `ffmpeg` and `ffprobe` on PATH; `uv run` installs the Python side. Recordings come from `screen-record`.

```
uv run <skill>/scripts/annotate.py VIDEO -o OUT --start HH:MM:SS[.fff] --events events.json \
    [--steps steps.json] [--masks masks.json] [--from T --to T] [--frames T,T,...] [--offset S] [--idle-speed N [--idle-after S]] [--font FILE] [--no-video]
```

All times are wall-clock times of the same clock. `--start` is the wall time of the video's first frame (`screen-record`'s `stop` prints it); `--from`/`--to` cut a clip (`--from` earlier than `--start` is an error: the video has nothing there); `--frames` also saves those annotated frames as PNG beside `OUT`, named with millisecond time; a time outside the cut is skipped with a warning. `--no-video` writes only those stills (a still = `--frames T --no-video`, cut with `--from/--to` so the scan stays short). `--offset S` is the calibration knob: seconds added to every event time. `--idle-speed N` plays every quiet stretch longer than `--idle-after` seconds (default 2) N times faster, the corner saying 快轉 N×: quiet means no glide, press or ring (0.8 s before a press to 1 s after), no key pill and no step caption in its first 1.5 s; annotations keep their wall-clock timing. `--font` takes a CJK font file; without it the first of the usual Windows and Linux ones is used. The encoder is NVENC when a one-frame probe encode works, else libx264. Examples of all three files: `examples/`.

- `events.json`: `{"space":[w,h],"pointer":"touch|mouse|none","style":{...},"events":[{"t":"HH:MM:SS.fff","kind":"tap|swipe|down|up","x":..,"y":..,"x2":..,"y2":..,"ms":..}]}`. `space` is two numbers, cast to integers. Coordinates are in `space` (the app's own pixel grid) and map to the frame by its size, so a 1920x1080 log works on a 1280x720 clip. `swipe` runs `x,y` to `x2,y2` over `ms`; `down`/`up` bracket a press (`up` may carry the release point `x,y`); a press without movement past 0.3 s shows as a hold. Keys go in the same list: `{"t","kind":"key","keys":"Ctrl+S"}` shows keycaps for a second, `{"t","kind":"type","text":"...","ms"?}` reveals the text (45 ms a character unless `ms` gives the total) in a pill that later typing within 1.2 s joins; the pill sits bottom centre, above a step caption. One finger at a time; a stray `up` is ignored; a tap or swipe missing a coordinate is an error naming it. `pointer` (default `touch`) picks what marks the contact: `touch` a fingertip dot that appears on the press (emulators, phones), `mouse` an arrow that glides to each press and shrinks while pressing (desktop or browser runs recorded without the cursor), `none` only the rings and trails (the recording shows the real cursor: `screen-record --cursor`). `style` (every key optional) sets the look: `cursor` `mac` (default, black arrow) / `cua` (Cua Driver's own cursor, with its glow) / `figma` (small coloured arrow); `color` `#rrggbb` for rings, trails, effects and the `cua`/`figma` pointer and name tag (default `#5ec0e8` for `cua`, else `#4785ff`; one colour per actor reads well); `label` a name tag beside the pointer (default none); `trail` the drag trail (default `true`); `motion` how the mouse pointer glides between presses, Cua Driver's styles ported in `scripts/cursor_motion.py`: `signature_arc` (default: one arc with a small follow-through, a speed glow), `spring_settle` (lands with a soft bounce, a speed glow), `comet_swoop` (a wider arc with a comet trail); `arc` scales the arc (`0.25` = the style's own, `0` straight); `tilt` how far the tip turns toward the motion (`1` = as Cua, `0` upright); `glow` the speed glow. Their defaults follow the cursor: `cua` swings as Cua does (`0.25`, `1`, `true`), `mac` and `figma` stay calm (`0.1`, `0`, `false`). A glide ends as the next press starts; a gap shorter than the move plays it faster. `zoom` (default `0`, off; `true` = 1.5, Cap's default) turns on the auto camera, a spring with Cap's constants (settles in about 0.55 s): presses group while they fit one view at that magnification (a drag fits its whole path, wider if needed); a group of two or more presses, a drag, or a press alone in its run of activity (gaps under 2.5 s) zooms in 0.6 s before its first press and pans to the next group, while a lone press amid scattered ones keeps the full frame, so busy, spread-out work stays wide instead of pumping in and out; typing keeps the view, and the camera eases out where it is a second after a group; a step's own `zoom` wins. `idle_fade` (seconds, default `0` = off) fades a resting mouse pointer out after that long and back in a quarter second before it moves; `float` (default `false`) adds Cua's slow resting drift. Both leave the press point itself exact. `caption` picks the step caption: `pill` (default: a translucent capsule bottom centre, "n/total │ title"; `dot: true` adds an amber dot in front), `chapter` (the same capsule top left), `callout` (a white card beside the step's first press, else its first box, with an arrow; it takes the side away from the pointer and its name tag, and falls back to `pill` without an anchor). `frame` (default none) puts the video on a background, `"#rrggbb"` or a diagonal gradient `["#6d5dfc","#f27b9b"]`, with padding, rounded corners and a shadow; the output grows by the padding, so the recording keeps its full size. An unknown key or value is an error.
- `steps.json`: `[{"from","to","title","note"?,"boxes"?:[[x,y,w,h,"label"]],"zoom"?:[x,y,w,h]}]` in the events' space. Steps are numbered in list order; the video shows only the title, so keep it short, and `note` is for the report, not drawn; `zoom` eases into that region for the step and back out.
- `masks.json`: `{"space":[w,h],"masks":[{"template":"<png>","threshold":0.8,"boxes":[[x,y,w,h]]}]}`. A box is blacked out while its template (cropped from a frame, at `space` scale) matches anywhere in the frame: scanned at 2 fps, each hit covers ±1 s. Template paths are relative to the masks file; a box that reaches outside the frame is clamped to it; a box with a width or height of 0 or less is an error.

1. **Collect the events** from whatever the project logs (input log, test harness, tool trace) into `events.json`, in the log's own coordinate space; a script converting the log is the project's, not this skill's. Done: every gesture to show is an entry, and `space` is the grid the log used.
2. **Write the steps** for the moments the viewer must follow, one caption each, boxes around what to look at. Done: each step's `from`/`to` lies inside the clip.
3. **Write the masks before anything is shared.** Crop one template per private screen (a stable element that appears only with the private data) and box the data. Done: a `--frames` still taken on each such screen shows the boxes black; a screen the templates do not cover is a leak, so add a template.
4. **Render a short cut first** (`--from/--to` around two or three steps, `--frames` inside them) and open the stills. Done: taps land on what was tapped; a consistent early or late drift is fixed with `--offset`, not by editing events.
5. **Render the full clip**, or the stills only. Done: `OUT` plays and its length equals the cut (less what `--idle-speed` saved).

Boxes drawn on a recording are annotation: a recording that carries private data stays unshared until step 3 passes on its stills.
